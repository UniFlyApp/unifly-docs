# Virtual airlines & hangars

## Your hangar

Keep the aircraft you fly in your own hangar, from your account (**Account → Flights**). Add each aircraft by its
registration, with its type, an optional name and livery, and its home base. You can have up to 50.

- **Registrations are unique** on UniFly, first come first served (G-ABCD and GABCD are the same).
- **Every aircraft gets an MSN** when it's added, which it keeps for life, and its hours belong to the airframe. If you
  re-register it, its hours stay and the old registration is listed under *Previously*.
- **Retiring an aircraft** keeps its history. Its registration is free for someone else 30 days later.
- **Flying your aircraft:** pick the aircraft for a flight in your logbook (✈ Aircraft), or choose **I'm flying** in
  your own flight's panel and that flight, and the ones after it, are logged against it until you change it.
- **Aircraft pages:** tap an aircraft for its flights, hours, landings, distance, best landing, where it's parked now,
  the airports it's been to, and who's flown it.
- A pilot's aircraft are on the **Hangar** tab of their pilot page, and an airport's **Traffic** tab lists the
  aircraft parked there.

> **Your hangar and a virtual airline don't mix.** Each flight belongs to one aircraft: either one of yours or one of
> an airline's, never both.
>
> - Fly with the callsign of an airline you're a member of and the flight is on the airline's aircraft. Your flight
>   panel doesn't offer your own aircraft (*I'm flying*): it says the flight's on the airline's aircraft instead.
> - A flight like that is **never** logged on one of your own aircraft, even if it doesn't count for the airline (no
>   aircraft of that type parked there, say), and even if you'd already chosen *I'm flying* for one of yours. Your pick
>   isn't lost: it carries on to your next flight under a callsign that isn't your airline's.
> - A VA flight stays with the airline's aircraft, so it can't be moved onto one of your own aircraft in your logbook
>   afterwards.
> - Your hangar aircraft can't fly for an airline or be moved into its fleet. The airline gets its own aircraft
>   (from the 🏪 Aircraft market).
> - Registrations are unique across UniFly, so the same registration can't be in your hangar and in an airline's fleet.

## Virtual airlines (alpha)

> Virtual airlines are in **alpha**. Things will change as we go, and prices and balances may be rebalanced, but
> airlines won't be wiped. Because the numbers change often, this page explains how things work rather than listing
> prices. Each airline's own pages show the current figures, with short how-to guides.

Open the 🏢 button to browse the virtual airlines (with their home base, fleet, members and recent flights), search
them, or start your own.

### Starting an airline

Each pilot can start one airline: a name, a 3 to 5 letter code (the callsign it flies under), a radio callsign, a
colour and a short description. A new airline gets a starting grant from the UniFly Authority (see below), or can take
a startup loan instead. A checklist on its Overview walks you through the first steps: your first aircraft, a timetable
flight, a second pilot and your first flights.

An airline using a real airline's name or code is only visible to its owner until the UniFly Team has seen proof the
real airline allows it. While it's waiting, the owner can withdraw it and start again. Airline names and codes (like
flight party names, event requests and registrations) are checked for rude words.

### Joining and roles

Airlines are either open (join straight away) or by application (the owner or staff accept or decline). Roles are
owner, staff and member, and owners can create positions with their own powers, such as looking after the money,
Marketing (campaigns and fares) or Timetable (scheduled flights), so an airline can have a Route Planner and a separate
Head of Marketing. Every pilot builds a **career** with their airline: flights, hours, passengers, on-time record, landings
and a rank by hours flown for it.

If an owner hasn't been on UniFly for 180 days, the staff can take the airline over.

### Flying for your airline

There's no booking needed and nothing to click:

1. Connect with your airline's code and a flight number as your callsign (ESX123, say).
2. Fly an aircraft type the airline has parked at the airport you take off from.
3. The map picks the aircraft, counts the flight for the airline, and moves the aircraft to where you land.

If there's no aircraft of that type there, the flight doesn't count for the airline. A few extras:

- **Fly this one:** with more than one suitable aircraft at an airport, tap 🎟 *Fly this one* on its fleet card to
  pick it for your next flight.
- **Timetable:** members can book upcoming timetable flights. A booked flight is yours, and its aircraft is picked for
  you an hour before departure. A timetable flight being flown right now shows ✈️ *In the air*, with who's flying it,
  how far along it is, the time to go and *Show on the map*.
- **Any callsign:** after *Fly this one* or a booking, you can connect as any callsign (to get another airline's
  livery in your sim) and the flight still counts.
- **Ferry flights:** a flight number from 9900 to 9999 is a ferry. It moves the aircraft but doesn't earn. Aircraft
  away from home are listed under *Needs ferrying*. Owners and staff can also 🚚 **Move** an aircraft without flying it,
  but a hired crew costs more than flying it yourself, and the price is shown before anything's charged.
- **Wrong aircraft?** For 24 hours after landing, a flight can be switched to the other aircraft it could have been.

### Running the business

Airlines earn and spend UniFly credits (UC):

- **Money in:** passenger fares and cargo on each flight, onboard sales (food, drinks and duty free, which matter
  most to Budget airlines; Premium airlines include it in the ticket), paid Wi-Fi, contracts, and prizes.
- **Money out:** fuel (based on the real jet fuel price, changing each Monday, and cheaper at big hubs), crew and upkeep, airport
  and passenger charges, landing fees, leases, maintenance checks, base staff and the Authority's tax.
- **Each flight's report** shows its money line by line, its fuel burned, and a passenger review.

What owners and staff can do:

- **Fleet:** lease or buy aircraft from the **🏪 Aircraft market**: new ones (built to order), used ones from UniFly
  Leasing's stock, or ones other airlines are selling. Newer-generation types cost more but burn less; classics are
  cheaper but thirstier. Flying fewer aircraft families keeps costs down.
- **Maintenance:** each aircraft is a real airframe with A, C and D checks, done at the airline's bases. Very hard
  landings mean an inspection. Airframes have a working life, which can be extended once.
- **Bases, routes and fares:** open bases, plan routes (the route planner estimates the profit for each aircraft type),
  and set fares per route and cabin (Saver, Standard or Premium). Rivals' fares are shown. Lay out cabins and cargo
  holds, and pick a service level (Budget, Standard or Premium).
- **Wi-Fi:** fit it to an aircraft from the Fleet tab (a one-off cost by size, then a monthly fee). In Settings, make
  it paid (passengers buy it on flights over an hour; First is always free) or free for everyone (no takings, but better
  reviews and passengers coming back on the route). Long-haul passengers notice when there's none.
- **Marketing:** campaigns to grow the airline's name.
- **Contracts:** 📋 charters and cargo jobs, with the pay on top of the flight's own earnings. The board has jobs in four
  sizes: Light (bizjets and turboprops, such as an urgent spare part or a medical team), Small (regional aircraft),
  Medium (narrowbodies) and Large (widebodies). *Fits my fleet* shows only the jobs your aircraft have the seats or
  payload and range for. Each size also has a **regular contract**: fly a route a set number of times within one to
  three weeks, paid each flight, with a bonus for flying them all. Unclaimed jobs leave the board after a couple of days.
- **Fuel price locks** for a few weeks, and **loans** from the UniFly Authority.
- **Wet leases:** hire another airline's aircraft with its crew, or offer yours.
- **Mergers and takeovers:** two airlines can become one, with the UniFly Team's approval.

What helps an airline do well: smooth landings, being on time, flying routes regularly (loyal passengers), connecting
passengers at your bases, and a good **safety grade** (A to D, from the last 90 days' landings). Long delays mean paying
passengers compensation.

When a cabin's full, or the aircraft doesn't have that cabin, passengers who'd have been turned away are **upgraded
free** to an empty seat in the cabin above, and pay the fare they booked. The flight's report says how many were
upgraded.

### The UniFly Authority

The Authority is the virtual airlines' bank, run by the rules rather than by a person. It pays the starting grants,
collects tax on every flight's earnings, lends money, and runs UniFly Leasing (the market's own aircraft). Its page
(🏛 in the 🏢 window) shows its balance, the rules and the loans open.

An airline that runs out of money is warned and has time to recover (a rescue loan may be possible if it's still
flying). If it can't, it goes **bankrupt**: its aircraft go to the market and its flights stop counting. Its owner can
relaunch it, with the same code and members.

### The airline's page

- **Overview:** "Fly for…" near the top, its standing (safety grade and value), a pinned notice for pilots, *Needs
  doing* for owners and staff, the latest news and busiest routes, badges, passenger reviews and the weekly report
  (which can also go to your own Discord via a webhook).
- **News:** everything that's happened at the airline.
- **Routes:** every route it flies, with its distance and how well known the airline is on it, the button to show them
  on the map, and the route planner.
- **Fleet:** each aircraft as a card, with where it is, its hours, checks, cabin and range, and a progress bar while
  it's flying.
- **Pilots:** a table of each pilot's role, rank, flights, hours, last flight and when they joined. Tap a column to sort
  it.
- **Money:** this month in and out, and **who earns what**: each aircraft's and pilot's flights, hours and earnings, all
  time or the last 30 days.
- **Flights, Marketing and Settings**, depending on your role.

An airline's **value** is its cash and the aircraft it owns, less what it owes, plus six months of its recent profit.
That profit counts the monthly bills (leases, insurance, staff and bases), but not grants, loans or aircraft bought or
sold.

### The leaderboard

🏆 in the 🏢 window ranks airlines over the last 30 days by flights, hours, passengers, cargo, on-time, smooth landings,
pilots flying, value and safety. Each month the top three by hours flown (then flights) are named **airline of the
month**, win a prize from the Authority, and are posted to Discord. Airlines also earn their own badges.
